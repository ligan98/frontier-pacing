Pacing the AI Frontier: Interactive Companions

Li Gan

A few companies are building ever more capable AI. Today's models already write code and help with research. In scenarios such as the AI Futures Project's, within a few years models will do most AI research themselves. Faster research makes better models, and better models make research faster, so within a year or two the frontier could jump to AI that beats top experts in every field. This fast stretch is the takeoff period, and all four papers study it.

The difficulty is that the stronger the model, the worse and the harder to undo a loss of control would be. Think of frontier development as a race car. The engine is capability research, which makes models stronger. The brake is safety research, which makes them reliable and controllable. The track gets more dangerous toward the end, and a crash ends the race for everyone. The papers share one message. Stop and build a good brake before racing again, because a fast car that cannot stop is not worth having sooner. The first paper shows why the brake should come first, and the earlier the better. The second asks who pays for the brake. The third explains why competing labs will not stop on their own, and the fourth shows how two countries can agree to stop together.

The four papers share one model. A lab's compute has three uses: serving paying customers, capability research and safety research. Compute doubles every six months, and research speeds up as models improve. Safety knowledge lowers the monthly risk of catastrophe at each capability level. In the AI Futures Project's worked example, the takeoff period takes about 18 months at full speed. The numbers are illustrative. They compare rules and are not estimates of real risk.

One page per paper. The model runs in your browser, so moving a slider shows the result at once.

  1. 1Risk Ceilings and Development DeadlinesPausing capability research at the start of the takeoff period and putting the compute into safety research cuts the risk of catastrophe sharply, and the earlier the pause, the better.
  2. 2Safety Research and the Allocation of Frontier ComputeIn the baseline, customers should pay for extra safety compute when reaching the end of the takeoff period raises their services by more than 34 percent; otherwise capability research should pay a share too.
  3. 3Prizes, Shared Hazard, and the Speed of Frontier AISpeeding up raises a lab's own chance of winning the prize from leading, while all labs share the added risk of catastrophe, so competing labs develop far faster than a common rule would choose, and one lab's restraint achieves much less than a common rule.
  4. 4Agreements Have a ClockIf both countries pause capability research and keep doing safety research, the global probability of catastrophe falls sharply. A country that pauses alone is soon overtaken, while a joint pause can last much longer, stretching the takeoff period and giving safety research more time to catch up.
